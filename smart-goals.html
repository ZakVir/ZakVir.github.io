SMART Goals

Summary. Goals written in the SMART format: Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, and Time-bound. The document is titled “SMART Goals for Introduction to Engineering Design.” My three goals are:

  1. Introduction to Engineering Design: be able to create 3D-printed designs. I will practise once a week for 30 minutes with what I currently know, and I will know I have accomplished it by making 10 3D models by myself.
  2. Science and Engineering Learning Community: learn how to incorporate science in engineering. I will do all my work on time and try to understand all the material properly, and I will know I have completed this goal when I finish the SELC Program.
  3. School year: have good grades for my first year in high school — a minimum of 2 B’s and the rest A’s. I will study a week in advance for tests and quizzes and complete all my homework on time.

Reflection

Why this demonstrates achievement. Each goal has a clear target and a way to tell whether it is done: 10 models made by myself, finishing the SELC Program, and at most 2 B’s with the rest A’s. That is the planning skill behind the engineering work in my Arduino project: before building, I decide what success looks like and how I will know I reached it. The goals use frequencies and milestones rather than calendar dates — practising once a week, studying a week in advance — so the next step is to keep that weekly practice going, including the 3D-printing practice in Goal 1.
