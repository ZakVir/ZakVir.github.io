UNIT 2 Self-Assessment: Grit Scale

Summary. A self-assessment for Unit 2 of Dollars & Sense, using Angela Duckworth’s Grit Scale (adapted). Grit means passion plus perseverance, and it is not a fixed trait — it can be deliberately developed. I rated 10 statements from 1 (not like me at all) to 5 (very much like me). The perseverance items are 2, 4, 7, 8, 9, and 10; the passion and focus items (1, 3, 5, 6) are reverse-scored. All 10 scores are added and divided by 10 for the Grit Score, out of 5.0. A score of 3.8 or higher is in the top quartile.

My Grit Score: 2.85 / 5.0 (as calculated on the sheet; top quartile is 3.8+).

Reflection

What the score shows. My strongest marks are for hard work, diligence, and finishing what I begin — I rated myself 4 out of 5 on being a hard worker, finishing whatever I begin, and being diligent and never giving up. My lowest mark is for having achieved a goal that took years of work, which makes sense in my first year of high school: I have not had years yet. I also marked myself in the middle on new ideas distracting me, and lower on staying obsessed with one idea — so focus over months is the part to work on.

Why this demonstrates achievement. This is an honest baseline, not a perfect score, and that is the point of a self-assessment: it shows where I am starting from. Grit can be deliberately developed, and my SMART goals are the plan for doing it — practising 3D printing once a week, doing all my work on time, and studying a week in advance. The next time I take this scale, the comparison will show whether the plan worked.
